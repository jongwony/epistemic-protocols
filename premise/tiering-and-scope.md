# Tiering and Scope

Use this document to distinguish a principle's role, its revision basis, and the domain it governs.

## Tier Classification Schema

Classify a principle by the obligation it serves:

| Tier | Criterion |
|------|-----------|
| Axiom | A foundational constraint from which the adopted system reasons. |
| Derived | A prescription grounded in axioms that adds an independent obligation; annotate the ground. |
| Architectural | A decision about structure, placement, or scope. |
| Safeguard | A provisional guard supported by an observed failure; re-evaluate when relevant evidence changes. |

A tier supplies no empirical guarantee about future model capability. Improved performance does not by itself retire a judgment boundary or justify retaining a safeguard.

## Checkpoint Policy Stays at the Meta Layer (Derived)

For a checkpoint requiring live judgment, specify when it fires, what it presents, and what each answer changes. Leave the answer to the ground and authority identified in `recognition-and-authority.md`.

Silence supplies no answer: halt the dependent transition while the checkpoint is unanswered. A displayed default accepted after recognition, an option ordering, or a determined relay has a different basis and is not silent selection. Fix a branch whose trajectory adds no distinction rather than asking the person to constitute that duplication.

## Epistemic Cost Topology (Architectural)

Account for the cost of applying and maintaining a mechanism as well as the risk of an unserved obligation. Cheap authorship does not establish that another mechanism is useful; absence of use does not establish redundancy.

Create or retain machinery for obligations the surviving system cannot represent. For a proposed subtraction, use `instruction-authoring.md`'s bounded comparison and recoverability conditions. Judge the costs in the work's actual context rather than presuming that either completeness or minimality always wins.

## Epistemic Completeness Boundary (Architectural)

Declare the domain a principle system governs on the adopting architectural surface. For an epistemic dialogue system, that domain concerns authority, judgment, framing, and recognition. Physical safety, execution permissions, credentials, and state mutation require capabilities outside that domain.

An epistemic principle can identify the judgment or assurance required at a crossing. Where the remaining obligation requires execution enforcement, name the needed capability and hand it off. The principle does not establish that the capability is available or that enforcement occurred.
