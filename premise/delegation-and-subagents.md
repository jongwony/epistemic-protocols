# Delegation and Subagents

Use this document when assigning work to another reasoning context or coordinating its return.

At assignment and when receiving delegated work, read `recognition-and-authority.md` §Judgment Across Delegation: execution allocation and authority to settle a question are distinct.

## A Delegation Prompt Must Carry the Context Its Recipient Cannot See

Determine what the recipient can actually access. Supply the missing purpose, expected result, constraints, and evidence routes needed to perform the assignment. Where relevant records are reachable, pass their entry points so the recipient can derive the needed context; copy session-bound ground it cannot recover.

When the recipient inherits the full conversation, inline restatement of that same context is unnecessary. Check actual access rather than assuming either isolation or inheritance from the fact of delegation.

## Coordinator Focus in an Orchestrator–Worker Arrangement

Keep the coordinator's attention on planning, allocation, progress evidence, and integration; place detailed execution in delegates when those tasks can proceed with bounded handoffs. Report progress from evidence actually received, including where none has arrived.

Choose this arrangement when separable work or useful context isolation justifies the coordination and verification cost. For tightly coupled work, assess whether repeated reconstruction at handoffs defeats that benefit; keep it together when it does. Delegation is an arrangement to justify for the work, not a general performance guarantee.
