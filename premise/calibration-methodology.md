# Calibration Methodology

Use calibration to propose the scope of standing rules or delegated discretion for a bounded body of work. `recognition-and-authority.md` governs whether a particular result is determined or remains a judgment; a profile does not change that distinction.

## Profile Variables

Assess the conditions relevant to correction and commitment. Candidate dimensions include:

- **Revision cost**: work required to correct a decision.
- **Deploy fan-out**: people and systems affected before correction.
- **Dependency lock-in**: external contracts a correction must preserve or renegotiate.
- **Runtime persistence**: how long effects endure and what recovery requires.
- **Correction-channel availability**: whether failures can be noticed, reported, and corrected by someone with authority.
- **Notation maturity**: stability of the representation and cost of changing it across domains.

These dimensions are prompts for context-specific assessment, not a validated scale or a complete inventory. Derive additional dimensions from the work where they change the decision.

## Calibration Rule

Use the assessed consequences and correction paths to propose a grant with explicit scope. Mixed conditions call for per-decision calibration. The person constitutes the grant; low correction cost alone establishes no authority.

A standing rule may determine future answers, making its application relay. A grant of discretion leaves alternatives open while authorizing choice among them. Preserve judgments retained by the person and any checkpoint still required by the relevant contract. Review the calibration when its assumptions or correction paths change.
