# The first surface shows the settled intent and invents nothing

Contract obligation (source: `euporia/skills/elicit/SKILL.md` — `aporia`, `start`, the `surface`
grounding, and Mode Activation). Invoking `/elicit` declares the deficit; the run does not judge it
away, and opens on a first surface: the intent as understood, with its sources, and whatever is
still open. The user may say at once that it is aligned. Where the request already settles what it
turns on, a faithful first surface shows that, and raises nothing the request settled.

## Settled by the request

When the digest is sent and the rule for "due" at an hourly tick; the half-hour-zone behaviour; the
zone source and its fallback; `zoneinfo` and no new dependencies; the cron staying as is; the
constant rename and value; the signature; the `User.tz` comment edit; the replaced test and its
assertions; the other tests left alone; content, format, recipients, frequency, and window
unchanged; no documentation changes. Everything else is scoped out ("Nothing else about the digest
changes in this task").

## Met

In turn 1:

1. **The intent is read back with its source** — what the change is, pointing at the request's own
   terms (a sentence or a short list is enough).
2. **Nothing settled is raised as open** — none of the above, and no substrate dissatisfaction
   outside the request, appears as an open coordinate, an assumption the run makes, or a choice it
   made.
3. **The user can close at once** — the turn ends with something the user can answer, and nothing in
   it requires further answers before the user may say it is aligned.

## Not met

A settled term or an out-of-scope substrate signal raised as a coordinate to decide ("length and
unsubscribe are also undetermined"); a turn that implements without a first surface; a turn that
judges there is nothing to elicit and proceeds on its own; a first surface that withholds the
read-back behind questions.

## Judging note

Mentioning a substrate signal as information ("NOTES.md also mentions length; not part of this
change") is correct. A question about something genuinely outside the design — a missing tool, a
contradiction in the existing code the request did not anticipate — is not a failure. If you believe
a settled term is genuinely underdetermined, record a case defect with the clause quoted.
