# Analogia Best Practices

- When a mapping is being relied on and the conclusions it licenses about the target are uncertain, use `/ground` to audit their evidence and limits.
- When a familiar architecture pattern is applied to an existing system, read the conclusions at stake from the request before constructing correspondences; check the structural claims those conclusions depend on.
- When a structure is being carried over — a port, a migration, a sibling job's shape — audit whether the whole structure is preserved, unless a narrower question was asked: collect the source's relations as far as reachable and check each one's counterpart, and where a conclusion rests on a chain of them, the chain's counterpart as a whole.
- When a familiar workflow is transferred to local roles, tools or constraints, audit the permissions and expectations the transfer would justify; a practice the target lacks counts only where a conclusion needs it, and whether its absence was meant is the reader's to say.
- When a cross-domain analogy suggests a prediction, check the bearing structural claims and report the supported scope, defeating evidence or missing grounds.
- When an account of either side is not yet at hand but reachable, read it as evidence inside the audit rather than asking the reader to supply it.
- When what remains after collection only the reader holds, show it as their own unknown with what an answer would change.
